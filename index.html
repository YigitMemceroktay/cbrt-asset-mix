    Gold and securities as a share of assets

    For most of the 1990s and 2000s gold was a minor line, between 2% and 7% of assets and as low as 1.8% in December 2001. It began climbing in 2013, reached 27% by the end of 2020 and hit a record 44.4% in March 2026. On 18 September 2026 it stands at 37.9%.

    Securities went the other way. TRY government bonds issued to the bank after the 2001 crisis were 45% of assets in April 2002; since 2013 they have stayed between 1.6% and 8.8%. Foreign-currency securities, where the bank invested most of its FX reserves, were 68% of assets in 2013. They fell to 1.5% by the end of 2023, recovered to 14% in March 2025 and are 3.7% now.

      What the balance sheet is made of since 2013

      The weekly statement's current format, available from January 2013, breaks the asset side down finely enough to follow each block. In January 2013 foreign-currency securities were 68% of assets and gold 14%. By September 2026 gold is the largest block at 38%, followed by accumulated losses carried as an asset (18%), other FX reserves such as deposits abroad (18%) and lending to banks (13%). TRY government bonds have been a thin layer throughout, at 2% to 4%.

        How much of the gold is the bank's own

        The 794 tonnes on the balance sheet are not all the CBRT's. Banks can hold part of their reserve requirements in gold (213 t), deposit gold or pledge it as collateral (43 t), the Treasury keeps gold at the bank (23 t) and 30 t is owed to foreign banks. Gold not matched by any of these gold liabilities is 485 t, down from a peak of 616 t in March 2023. From 2013 to 2017 it sat at 119 t; all the movement came from banks' gold. In dollar terms gold is now 64% of official reserves, against 3% to 6% through the 2000s.

            The TRY bond portfolio and outright purchases

            The CBRT holds TRY 448 bn of TRY government bonds in the weekly statement of 18 September 2026 (TRY 466 bn in the analytic balance sheet of 28 September). The portfolio grows in steps that line up with purchase auctions: TRY 77 bn bought in 2020, TRY 188 bn across 2022 and 2023, TRY 121 bn in April 2025 alone and TRY 302 bn so far in 2026, of which TRY 143 bn came in March. The bank has not sold bonds outright since 1996. The portfolio shrinks only as bonds mature, as in 2024, when it fell by TRY 21 bn despite TRY 10 bn of purchases.

              Sources and method

              Total assets

              Gross total assets are the “Total assets” line of the CBRT weekly statement (EVDS TP.BL055, archive TP.AB.K065). They include accounting entries on the asset side: previous years' losses (A17, on the latest date), the loss of the period and the revaluation account. The “Excluding losses & revaluation” option removes these three.

              Gold

              A1 Gold (international standard, TP.BL001) plus non-international-standard gold held under other assets (TP.BL045); archive TP.AB.K001 before 2013. Tonnes come from the net-gram lines. “Not matched by gold liabilities” subtracts the gram lines for banks' reserve requirements in gold, other bank gold deposits and collateral, Treasury gold and gold owed to foreign banks. It is a balance-sheet estimate, not an official figure for the bank's own gold, and it does not capture off-balance-sheet swap positions.

              TRY government securities

              From 17 August 2001, the analytic balance sheet line A.2Aa1 Securities (TP.AB.A051). Since 2013 it matches the weekly statement's A5 Securities portfolio to the lira in every week. Before August 2001 the analytic balance sheet is monthly or missing, so the chart uses the archive statement's A5 Securities portfolio, which is defined differently (it moves with repo operations in some years). The chart marks the switch.

              Foreign-currency securities

              A31 FX securities held with foreign correspondents (TP.BL005): the part of FX reserves invested in foreign bonds. The archive format (before 2013) does not report it separately, so the series starts in January 2013.

              Data handling

              • The new-format EVDS series carry one stray row dated 11 November 2011 whose values are inconsistent with the rest (implied USD/TRY of 6.1). It is dropped; the new format is used from 4 January 2013 and the archive until 28 December 2012.
              • All values are nominal TRY as published; shares are unaffected by inflation.
              • Official reserves are the CBRT's weekly gold and FX reserve figures in USD (bie_abres2).
